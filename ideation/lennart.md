# KBC Hackathon Challenge

## 1. Core problem

Historically, the challenge for customers was **finding information**.

Today, the challenge is **surviving information overload**:

- Applications
- Notifications
- Messages
- Social media
- Administrative tasks
- Banking and insurance interactions

Everyone is competing for the customer's attention.

As a result, one of the customer's scarcest resources is no longer money — it is **time**.

Every unnecessary click, repeated question, form, decision, and piece of complexity consumes that time.

---

## 2. KBC's perspective

Customers generally do not wake up wanting to:

- manage banking products;
- change payment limits;
- search for PIN codes;
- configure insurance;
- simulate loans;
- perform administrative tasks.

Customers have actual goals:

- Start a business
- Buy a home
- Travel
- Move house
- Manage their family
- Make an important purchase

**Banking and insurance are tools that help them achieve those goals.**

The experience should therefore minimize the effort required from the customer.

---

# 3. Hackathon challenge

> **Imagine KBC could understand and know what a customer wants or needs before they even ask for it.**

Design a concept showing how KBC could proactively help customers while reducing:

- complexity;
- customer effort;
- unnecessary interactions;
- frustration;
- time spent managing financial services.

The solution should help KBC identify the customer's situation and deliver the right service at the right moment.

---

# 4. Three required dimensions

Your solution should address three dimensions:

## A. UNDERSTAND

Determine:

> **What is the customer's current situation and likely intent?**

Potential signals could include:

- Customer behaviour
- Transactions
- Recent actions
- Changes in personal circumstances
- Product usage
- Location/context
- Previous interactions
- Life events
- Customer questions
- Existing KBC data

The objective is to infer what the customer may need next.

### Example

A customer changes their home address.

Instead of treating this simply as an address update, KBC could understand:

**Customer moved → customer may need to update or obtain home insurance.**

---

## B. ADAPT

Determine:

> **How should the customer experience change based on that understanding?**

The experience should dynamically adapt to the customer's situation.

Possible adaptations include:

- Proactively surfacing a relevant action
- Automatically preparing information
- Reducing the number of forms or clicks
- Pre-filling information
- Reordering relevant functionality
- Providing contextual recommendations
- Removing irrelevant steps
- Helping customers make decisions

### Example

Instead of requiring the customer to:

1. Change their address
2. Search for insurance
3. Determine whether existing insurance still applies
4. Enter information again

KBC could recognize the move and proactively ask:

> "We noticed you changed your address. Do you need home insurance for your new apartment?"

---

## C. SCALE

Determine:

> **How can this work across KBC rather than only for one isolated use case?**

The concept should potentially work across:

- Banking
- Insurance
- KBC Mobile
- Kate
- Different customer journeys
- Different products
- Different communication channels

KBC has approximately **2.5 million customers**, so the concept should not depend on manually designing every individual customer journey.

The idea should demonstrate a mechanism that can be generalized.

---

# 5. What KBC wants from the hackathon

KBC is **not simply asking for another feature**.

The team should deliver a:

## Proof of Concept + Vision

Show:

### 1. Customer signal
What information tells KBC something meaningful about the customer?

### 2. Understanding
What does KBC infer from those signals?

### 3. Action
How does the customer experience change?

### 4. Customer value
How does it save:

- Time
- Effort
- Complexity
- Frustration

### 5. Scalability
How could the same mechanism work for many customers, products, and situations?
